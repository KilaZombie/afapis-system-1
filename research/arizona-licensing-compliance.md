# Arizona licensing and compliance questions — System 1
Checked: 2026-11-08
Status: unresolved legal diligence; not legal advice.

## What official sources establish
- Arizona Registrar of Contractors says businesses contracting to build, alter, repair, improve, or perform covered mechanical/structural work must hold the appropriate contractor license. Source: https://roc.az.gov/applying-for-a-license
- ROC classifications include C-39 Air Conditioning and Refrigeration, whose scope includes installation, alteration, and repair of ventilation systems and duct work. Source: https://roc.az.gov/license-classifications
- ROC advises consumers to verify a contractor's license through the ROC rather than relying on advertising claims. Source: https://roc.az.gov/before-hire
- The EPA consumer guide has historically listed Arizona among states requiring special licensing for air-duct cleaning providers. The guide is old, so it is a signal to investigate, not a sufficient current legal determination. Source: https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/should-you-have-air-ducts-your-home-cleaned

## What remains unknown
The official ROC pages reviewed do not clearly state whether standalone cleaning-only services, without duct repair, alteration, installation, or mechanical work, require a particular ROC license in Arizona. Do not infer that C-39 is automatically required for every cleaning-only job, and do not infer that no license is required.

## Required control before partner approval
1. Ask the Arizona ROC for a written or clearly documented determination about standalone air-duct cleaning and dryer-vent cleaning, distinguishing cleaning-only work from repair, alteration, replacement, or HVAC work.
2. Check every prospect's exact legal business name and active license number in the ROC database when a license is required for its actual scope.
3. Obtain current insurance evidence directly from the business; ROC notes that licensing and insurance are separate questions.
4. Ensure the lead-generation site does not represent itself as the contractor, quote or contract for the work, dispatch technicians, or supervise the job.
5. Do not make health claims for duct cleaning. EPA says routine duct cleaning is generally not recommended and that evidence of health benefits is limited.

## Other relevant controls
- Google Business Profile says lead-generation agents or companies are ineligible for a Business Profile: https://support.google.com/business/answer/13762416
- Arizona Attorney General guidance says telemarketing calls and texts are subject to the Arizona Consumer Fraud Act and discusses Do-Not-Call permission issues: https://www.azag.gov/consumer/tips/robocalls-and-telemarketing
- Customer request form must disclose the recipient of the inquiry in plain language and capture auditable consent before sharing customer contact information. Do not use unsolicited automated calls or texts.
