# System 1 market validation — Phoenix metro
Date checked: 2026-11-08
Status: research hypothesis; not proof of demand, partner interest, or revenue.

## Executive finding
Keep System 1 as the first build candidate, but do not describe it as validated or income-producing. Phoenix has observable consumer demand and active competitors. The initial commercial bottleneck is not website production; it is obtaining at least one qualified contractor buyer and a reliable, compliant way to deliver and document leads.

## Current market evidence
- Angi's Phoenix dryer-vent cleaning guide, updated July 8, 2026, reports a typical range of $107–$156 and a $131 average; roof-access jobs can cost more. Source: https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-dryer-vent-cleaning-cost/az/phoenix
- Angi lists Phoenix air-duct cleaning and dryer-vent cleaning categories with substantial local review activity. Source: https://www.angi.com/companylist/us/az/phoenix/air-duct-cleaning.htm
- Public Phoenix offers are highly promotional. Groupon currently lists multiple duct/dryer-vent offers with steep advertised discounts. These offers are not reliable measures of final transaction value. Source: https://www.groupon.com/local/phoenix/dryer-vent-cleaning
- Local providers publish materially different prices: examples include $129 standard dryer-vent cleaning and $449 single-system duct cleaning at Proof Vent & Duct, and $499–$799 for a single-system duct cleaning at Desert Jade AC. These are seller-published prices, not independent market averages. Sources: https://proofvent.com/pricing/ and https://desertjadeac.com/services/duct-cleaning/
- Angi's local listings and public business listings show numerous established providers, including providers with hundreds of reviews. Competition is substantial.

## Compliance and platform constraints
- Google Business Profile explicitly lists lead-generation agents or companies as ineligible. Do not create a profile for this lead-generation operation. Source: https://support.google.com/business/answer/13762416
- Arizona Attorney General guidance states telemarketing calls and texts are subject to the Arizona Consumer Fraud Act and explains permission and Do-Not-Call concerns. No unsolicited automated calling or texting. Source: https://www.azag.gov/consumer/tips/robocalls-and-telemarketing
- The EPA says routine air-duct cleaning is generally not recommended absent specific conditions and warns against unsupported health claims. Content must be accurate and avoid claims that duct cleaning prevents illness or routinely improves health. Source: https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/should-you-have-air-ducts-your-home-cleaned

## Revenue model — hypotheses only
No lead price or buyer acceptance rate has been validated. Test exclusive, consented, service-specific leads with one contractor first.
Illustrative gross revenue arithmetic (not forecasts):
- 10 accepted leads/month × $25 = $250/month
- 30 accepted leads/month × $35 = $1,050/month
- 60 accepted leads/month × $40 = $2,400/month
- 100 accepted leads/month × $40 = $4,000/month
These figures exclude refunds, processing fees, taxes, rejected leads, and any platform charges. Do not incur any cost personally. Do not promise income.

## Build gates
1. Finish a genuinely useful local site and transparent lead form.
2. Use a zero-personal-cost lead-delivery method; verify limits and terms before relying on any free tier.
3. Add explicit, auditable consent for sharing the request with named/clearly described service providers; no resale to multiple buyers without separate clear consent.
4. Qualify a contractor buyer in writing: service area, job types, capacity, lead definition, price, response time, dispute/refund process, and data handling.
5. Run controlled end-to-end tests before accepting real customer data.
6. Only then seek approval to remove Netlify team SSO and publish publicly. Public exposure has not been authorized.
7. Track visitor-to-lead conversion, contact rate, accepted-lead rate, booked-job rate, buyer retention, revenue, refunds, and net margin.

## Zero-dollar authorization rule
Do not purchase or activate paid services, paid trials, domains, advertising, or subscriptions. Do not incur any charge or financial obligation. If a required capability cannot be delivered with a verified free option, stop that part and report the blocker. Revenue-funded fees may only be considered after the fee is disclosed and the user explicitly authorizes the business arrangement; they must never be charged to the user's personal funds.
