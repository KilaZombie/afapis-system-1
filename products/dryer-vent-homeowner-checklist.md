# Homeowner Dryer-Vent Care Checklist
## Phoenix-area homeowner edition

**Purpose:** A practical reminder sheet for recognizing when a clothes-dryer vent needs professional inspection or cleaning. This is general educational information, not a substitute for the appliance manufacturer's instructions or an on-site assessment.

### Warning signs to take seriously
- Clothes take noticeably longer than usual to dry.
- The dryer or laundry area becomes unusually hot during operation.
- There is a burning smell, visible lint around the dryer, or unusual heat at the exterior vent outlet.
- The exterior exhaust flap does not open normally while the dryer is running.
- The dryer shuts off unexpectedly or displays an airflow-related warning.

If you notice a burning smell, smoke, or signs of overheating, stop using the dryer. If there is smoke or fire, leave the area and call emergency services. Do not continue operating the appliance to test it.

### Routine homeowner habits
- Clean the lint screen before or after every load, following the appliance manual.
- Periodically inspect the visible area behind the dryer for crushed, kinked, or disconnected vent material. Do not move a heavy appliance if doing so is unsafe.
- Keep the exterior exhaust outlet clear of leaves, nests, landscaping, and other obstructions.
- Use only vent materials and configurations permitted by the dryer manufacturer and applicable local requirements.
- Do not operate the dryer without its lint screen installed.

### When to arrange a professional inspection
Arrange an inspection if drying performance changes, warning signs appear, the vent route is long or difficult to access, or you do not know when the vent was last inspected. Ask the provider to explain the scope of work, identify any damaged or unsuitable vent components, and show you the result where practical.

### Questions to ask a service provider
1. Do you inspect the full accessible vent route, including the exterior termination?
2. Is the quoted price for my specific vent layout, or could additional charges apply?
3. Will you identify damaged, disconnected, crushed, or non-compliant components separately from cleaning?
4. Are repairs included, or would they require a separately qualified professional?
5. What evidence will you provide that the work was completed?

### Important limitations
- There is no universal cleaning interval that fits every home. Follow the appliance manufacturer's guidance and respond to actual conditions.
- This checklist does not claim that routine duct or vent cleaning prevents illness or guarantees fire prevention.
- Do not attempt roof, wall-cavity, electrical, gas, or appliance repairs unless qualified to do so.
- A cleaning provider may not be authorized to perform HVAC, electrical, gas, or construction repairs. Verify licensing and insurance for the actual work being offered.
